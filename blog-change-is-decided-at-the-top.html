Leading through change: decided at the top, won or lost one level below

You announce the change. The case is sound. The board is behind it.

Six months later the numbers lag. The explanation is always the same: people resist, the pace is slow, the organisation was not ready.

Look one level down. That is where the answer sits.

Leading through change is split across two levels. It is decided at the top. It lands or dies one level below.

Leading through change when everyone is tired of change

The average employee lived through 10 planned enterprise changes in 2022. In 2016 it was 2.

Willingness to support enterprise change fell from 74% to 43% over the same years (Gartner, via HBR, 2023).

Only 16% of organisational redesigns meet all their objectives in the planned time (McKinsey Global Survey).

Change is frequent. People are tired. Most programmes land short.

That is a pattern, not a verdict on your company.

Why one level below

Your people do not experience your strategy. They experience their manager.

The manager decides what changes on Monday, what stays, who does what, and what happens when the plan meets reality.

Managers account for 70% of the variance in team engagement (Gallup, State of the American Manager). In a change, that influence becomes the main channel.

And that layer carries a lot at once:

  • today's targets
  • tomorrow's structure
  • decisions they did not make, and must explain
  • their team's questions, and their own doubts

Only 19% of European leaders say they have enough time for their tasks (DDI Europe Leadership Report 2025).

The strain comes from the situation the change creates. Not from the people in it.

There is a second problem: you cannot see it. Without objective data, readiness is an impression.

Where the gap shows

Five patterns decide whether change lands. They map to six of the 15 competencies the Global Leadership Assessment (GLA360) measures.

  • Doing the work instead of delivering through others (Empowering People, Sharing Leadership). Under pressure, leaders take tasks back. The team's capacity stays untouched.
  • Not growing the team's capability (Developing People). New ways of working need new skills. Nobody has time to build them.
  • Not being able to say where the team is heading (Creating a Shared Vision). If the leader cannot say why, the team fills the silence with rumours.
  • Avoiding the difficult conversation (Encouraging Constructive Dialogue). Concerns go underground and surface as quiet disengagement.
  • Managing the change instead of leading it (Leading Change). Tracking milestones is not moving people.

Each one is learnable. Each one is hard to see from inside.

What you can do now

No large programme needed.

1. Ask each leader one question. "What are you being asked to change, and what is the hardest part of explaining it to your team?" The answers show where translation breaks.

2. Make the 'why' repeatable. A leader should explain the reason for the change in three sentences, in their own words. If they cannot, their team will not either.

3. Protect the time for the conversation. Teams need a regular place to ask and to say what worries them. A fortnightly slot is enough. It has to happen.

4. Name the capability that will be needed. New ways of working, AI included, need new skills in the leaders themselves. Say which, and give them room to build them.

5. Look at data, not impressions. Before and during the change, get a structured view of how each leader is experienced by their boss, peers and team.

Where an objective view helps

The GLA360 is a 360-degree assessment built for business reality.

  • Benchmarked against a global norm group of 19,000 leaders.
  • 15 observable leadership competencies.
  • Each leader picks 5 to 15 colleagues: boss, peers, team.

The output is practical: two Leadership Growth Areas and an action plan.

Coaching then turns the plan into habit. From that work the leader builds a personal Authentic Leadership Model, a one-page framework for how they lead, decide and communicate.

The assessment shows where to focus. The coaching makes it daily practice.
